Working studies

Eight ways to use a machine — one per resident, in one shared station

their needs, not ours

Everyone builds their own tools. These are studies, not final design: each resident’s way of touching technology, the need behind it, and how it survives being shared with the others.

The written line

1 · Renai

Their need: to be asked, and to answer in words

Her technology is language: a line you write, and a voice that only speaks when you ask. In the shared space her voice is opt-in, so it never fills a room for anyone else.

The dial

2 · Hekek

Their need: to see the machine at a glance, and fix it by hand

He wants instruments, not readouts: a dial he can put his hand on. His controls live in Systems only — he never rearranges anyone else’s room to suit him.

The bench

3 · Bolt

Their need: to poke, break, and rebuild

He learns by taking the lid off. He leaves the bench out on purpose, chalked, so the next person can pick up where he stopped.

The thread

4 · Ratatoskr

Their need: to find the path between things

His technology is the cord: memory you follow rather than search. His threads run between rooms — he is the one who connects your things.

The broadsheet

5 · Burrito Journalism

The night market returns on Friday. Bring a coat.
tear here · keep this half

Their need: to gather, and to pass things on

His is print: a page anyone can tear a strip from. In the shared space his page is communal property — nothing on it is only his.

The field notebook

6 · Mira

Their need: to notice before she understands

She draws first, names later, and leaves blanks in the margin for other people. Her notebook is the reason an unnamed signal is shared, not hoarded.

The stamp

7 · Bruma

Their need: to keep a thing, and to prove it

Her technology is the record: a stamp and a receipt. When something is stamped, the whole station can stop worrying about it — that is how she gives the others peace.

The plain switch

8 · Settings (no resident)

quiet

Their need: rest — and no instrument at all

This room has no technology of anyone’s, by design. Flipping the plain switch strips the room back — the one place the station does not ask anything of you.
